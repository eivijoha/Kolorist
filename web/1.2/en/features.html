Colours and colour spaces

Every colour space

Edit colours in OKLCH, OKLab, CIE LCH, CIELab, Munsell, HSB, HSL, RGB and CMYK – with Display P3 as the wide gamut.

  • Display P3 side by side with sRGB, Adobe RGB, Rec. 2020, ProPhoto RGB, CMYK or any installed ICC profile
  • Enter CMYK or RGB directly in a chosen ICC profile
  • Save a colour in its colour model or in the profile – exports follow the format
  • Munsell in the steps of the Munsell book – hue 2.5, value 1 and chroma 2 – with Munsell notation New in 1.2
  • Warnings when a colour is out of gamut
  • Optionally limit colours to a chosen profile
  • Clean CMYK values: grey components move to black (UCR/GCR), with as few inks as possible

ICC profiles and colour libraries

Import your own ICC profiles (.icc, .icm) and colour libraries (.ase, .aco, .acb). They live in the app’s iCloud Drive folder and follow you to your other devices.

  • Convert between profiles with a chosen rendering intent
  • Investigate colour difference with ΔE2000
  • Colour charts with named tones: work within the chart, with the tone names shown
  • Import profiles and colour charts together, and delete them again in the app

Tones, harmonies and gradients

Tones and harmonies

Lighter and darker steps, tone scales from 50 to 950, and colour harmonies:

  • Complementary, split complementary, analogous, triad, square, double complementary and even distribution with a chosen count
  • Analogous with a complementary accent – a calm group and one colour that stands out New in 1.2
  • Natural lightness order: yellows lighter and blues darker, as in nature – or reversed for deliberate tension New in 1.2
  • On an OKLCH, CIE LCH, HSL or RYB colour wheel
  • The Munsell colour wheel with real Munsell colours, and Hering’s opponent-colour wheel
  • Saturation and lightness for the whole harmony, shown right in the colour wheel
  • See the whole harmony in the chosen colour space or colour library, with the base colour clearly marked
  • The colour-wheel values for each colour in the harmony – Munsell notation, OKLCH, LCH, HSL, RYB or Hering’s unique hues New in 1.2
  • Lighter and darker tones in equal or easing steps, shown in the swatches with values in the chosen colour model New in 1.2
  • Drag the base colour on the lightness scale to make the whole row lighter or darker New in 1.2

Gradients

Create transitions between two colours in equal perceptual steps in OKLab, with lighter and darker rows.

  • Export as a CSS gradient in oklab with an sRGB fallback
  • Linear, radial or conic
  • Save whole gradients and pick up where you left off
  • Add gradients to palettes
  • Copy the gradient straight into design, layout and presentation apps
  • The tones are shown in the swatches with values in the chosen colour model and colour space New in 1.2
  • Drag the base tones on the lightness scale to make the whole gradient lighter or darker New in 1.2

Getting colours

Pick colours

  • Camera with zoom, macro focus and torch – and a light panel on the Mac screen as a light source
  • Photos and images: zoom and dominant colours
  • Screen eyedropper on Mac
  • Colours as they really are: compensate for the light – also with a grey card or reference card in photos New in 1.2 Read more

Apple Intelligence on device

  • From value words to a palette, grounded in a knowledge base of more than a hundred colour concepts – nature, materials, styles and values
  • Describe a colour – “deep ocean blue”, “dusty pink” – and see it in Studio
  • Palettes are built on harmony principles with legible contrast
  • Adjust with free text – “warmer”, “more exclusive”
  • Name colours and get a critique of your palette

Everything runs on device. Nothing leaves it.

Contrast and colour vision

Text and graphics (WCAG 2.2)

  • WCAG 2.2 contrast: AA and AAA, large text and graphics
  • Auto-fix that adjusts the colour until it passes
  • Contrast matrix and critique of the whole palette
  • The critique shows what it is based on
  • The difference between two colours with ΔE2000

Surfaces and buildings (LRV)

  • Light reflectance value (LRV) and contrast between surfaces for buildings and interiors – BS 8300 and NS 11001
  • LRV and luminance contrast in the chosen light – including fluorescent and LED New in 1.2
  • Palette critique in different light: colours that change character, and colour pairs that become hard to tell apart New in 1.2

Colour vision

  • See the palette with protan, deutan and tritan deficiencies and achromatopsia, at any severity – and which colours become hard to tell apart
  • Colour vision deficiencies listed by how common they are, with estimated prevalence New in 1.2
  • Camera with a colour vision filter: see your surroundings as they may appear with each deficiency

Light and colour measurement New in 1.2

See colours and palettes in the light where they will be used, and pick colours as they really are. A grey card or a reference card turns your phone into a simple measuring tool for colour and light.

  • See colours and whole palettes in viewing conditions and standard viewing conditions
  • Compensate picked colours for the light – in photos too
  • Grey cards and reference cards for the right colour and lightness
  • Measure colour temperature, illuminance and estimated colour rendering
  • Import the reference values for your own card

Palettes, export and sharing

Palettes and iCloud

Collect colours in palettes, save single colours and whole gradients. Name them, move colours between palettes, and critique and check contrast for whole palettes.

  • Gradients in palettes
  • See whole palettes in a viewing condition New in 1.2
  • Print palettes on A4 – CIELab swatches with names and values
  • Undo what you do
  • Tailor the view to your work

Everything syncs through your own private iCloud.

Export

Adobe ASE, Photoshop ACO, Figma variables (DTCG JSON), Tokens Studio, SVG swatches, CSS, Design Tokens, GIMP GPL, SwiftUI and hex list.

Gradients: add gradients to palettes, and copy them as a gradient to Figma, Sketch, Affinity, Illustrator, InDesign, Photoshop, Pages, Keynote, Numbers, CSS or SwiftUI.

Print: palettes as A4 PDFs with swatches in CIELab (D50), names, the values in the colour model each colour was made in, the chosen colour space or colour library and the CIELab value.

Colours are exported in the format they were saved in – for example CMYK to Adobe.

Copy to … Figma, Illustrator, InDesign, Photoshop, Sketch and Affinity, Pages, Keynote and Numbers, CSS or SwiftUI – in the format each app accepts. Works for single colours and whole palettes.

On Mac, drag swatches straight into other apps – and drag colours into Kolorist from the colour panel and colour wells in other apps

Colour libraries: import your own colour charts in ASE, ACO or ACB (Adobe Color Book) with named tones, and show the nearest tones within them: colours, tones and harmonies thus stay on the set’s reference colours.

Sharing folder

  • Palettes are written as files to a folder in the cloud service you use, in the formats you choose New in 1.2
  • Kept up to date as palettes change – for colleagues on Windows too
  • The service’s own app uploads the files; Kolorist sends nothing itself

iPhone, iPad and Mac

Kolorist is designed for iPhone first. On iPad and Mac you get more room – in landscape on iPad and in a wide Mac window the colour swatches sit beside the tools New in 1.2 – and on the Mac an eyedropper that picks colours from anywhere on screen. On Mac and large iPads your palettes are at hand while you work, and you move colours where you need them with drag and drop. Colours you pick from the camera and photos are kept – clearly marked as not saved – until you decide.
