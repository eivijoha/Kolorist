Light and colour measurement

A colour is never just a colour – it is a surface in a light. Kolorist brings the light into the equation: see colours and whole palettes as they will appear where they are used, and pick colours from your surroundings as they really are – not as the evening light or a fluorescent tube makes them look. A grey card or a reference card turns your phone into a simple measuring tool for colour and light.

  • See colours in light

    Save viewing conditions – the living room in the evening, the office, the shop – or use standard viewing conditions for appraising prints and images (ISO 3664), workplaces and schools (EN 12464-1) and museums (CIE 157).

    • As the eye sees it: the eye adapts to the light, and dim light makes colours less colourful
    • As a photo: the full colour cast of the light
    • Whole palettes: colours that change character, and colour pairs that become hard to tell apart
    • LRV and luminance contrast in the chosen light, for buildings and interiors
    • Fluorescent and LED lights are calculated from their spectra

  • Colours as they really are

    Compensate the colours you pick for the light they were photographed in, so you get the colour the surface has in daylight.

    • From the light the camera measures, or a chosen light source
    • With a grey card or reference card for the right colour and lightness
    • In photos too: a card in the image is enough

  • The grey card as a yardstick

    An inexpensive grey card (18 %) or a white card gives both the colour and the strength of the light. The lightness comes out right, and you get colour temperature and illuminance (lux).

    • Custom card: use a grey or white surface with a known LRV, such as a painted wall

  • Reference cards for the highest accuracy

    With a 24-patch reference card in the image, Kolorist finds the card by itself, calibrates the camera against the reference values and shows how accurate the result is.

    • Calibrate the camera once – after that a grey card is enough, even in different light
    • Estimated colour rendering and the probable type of light
    • Accuracy given as mean and maximum ΔE00, cross-validated

  • Measure and document the light

    Colour temperature, illuminance and estimated colour rendering – useful when lighting conditions need documenting, or comparing with recommended levels, such as 500 lx at a workplace.

    • Save measured light as a viewing condition, and see other colours in it

  • Your own reference values

    Reference values for cards are not included with the app; you import the file that belongs to your card.

    • CGATS, CxF3, CSV/TSV and tables of Lab, XYZ or spectral values
    • Colour libraries (ASE, ACO, ACB)
    • With spectra, the reference values are calculated in any light

  • On Mac

    See colours and palettes in light, with the viewing conditions you measured on iPhone. With iPhone as the camera (Continuity), colours can be compensated with a grey card or reference card.

What do I need?

  • Nothing extra to see colours and palettes in light.
  • A grey or white card for compensation and light measurement.
  • A 24-patch reference card and the file with its reference values for the highest accuracy.
  • Light measurement requires iPhone or iPad.

How accurate is it?

A phone camera is not a measuring instrument. With a reference card the error is typically around 2–3 ΔE00 – the app shows the figure for each calibration. Illuminance and colour rendering are estimates, and colours without a measured spectrum get an estimated one.
