Colours and colour spaces

Every colour space

Edit colours in OKLCH, OKLab, CIE LCH, CIELab, Munsell, HSB, HSL, RGB and CMYK – with Display P3 as the wide gamut.

  • Display P3 side by side with sRGB, Adobe RGB, Rec. 2020, ProPhoto RGB, CMYK or any installed ICC profile
  • Choose the source colour space for CMYK and RGB – Generic CMYK and sRGB by default, or any ICC profile
  • Save colours in their colour model or profile – exports use the same format
  • Munsell in the steps of the Munsell book – hue 2.5, value 1 and chroma 2 – with Munsell notation
  • Warnings when a colour is out of gamut
  • Optionally limit colours to a chosen profile
  • Clean CMYK values: grey components move to black (UCR/GCR), with as few inks as possible
  • Viewing light: D50 (ICC standard), D65 or one of the viewing conditions
  • Absolute colorimetric keeps the paper white from the CMYK profile – 0/0/0/0 shows as the paper
  • CMYK in percent, and RGB as 0–255 in sRGB – the same numbers as in hex – and 0–1 in other colour spaces

ICC profiles and colour libraries

Import your own ICC profiles (.icc, .icm) and colour libraries (.ase, .aco, .acb). They live in the app’s iCloud Drive folder and follow you to your other devices.

  • Convert between profiles with a chosen rendering intent
  • Investigate colour difference with ΔE2000
  • Colour charts with named tones: work within the chart, with the tone names shown
  • Import profiles and colour charts together, and delete them again in the app

Filament colours for 3D printing

  • More than 2,200 filament colours from 150 manufacturers, in one library per material: PLA, PETG, ABS and ASA, TPU and TPE and others
  • Most measured with a colorimeter on printed samples; the rest estimated from photos and marked as such
  • Find the nearest filament to a colour, or lock palettes and harmonies to filaments that exist
  • Each colour links to the sample at FilamentColors.xyz (CC BY 4.0) – about the data
  • Read more about 3D printing and filament

Tones, harmonies and gradients

Tones and harmonies

Tone scales from 50 to 950, and colour harmonies:

  • Complementary, split complementary, analogous, triad, square, double complementary and even distribution with a chosen count
  • Monochromatic: one hue in tones along a line you shape freely in lightness and saturation
  • Tone path: tones along a path through lightness, saturation and hue – the hue arcs between two end points
  • Analogous with a complementary accent – a calm group and one colour that stands out
  • Natural lightness order: yellows lighter and blues darker, as in nature – or reversed for deliberate tension
  • On OKLCH, CIE LCH, HSL or RYB colour wheels
  • The Munsell colour wheel with real Munsell colours, and Hering’s opponent-colour wheel
  • Goethe’s colour wheel from his Theory of Colours (1810): purple, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet
  • Harmonies ordered from calm to high-contrast: one hue, neighbours, opposite hues and evenly around the wheel
  • Saturation and lightness for the whole harmony, shown right in the colour wheel
  • See the whole harmony in the chosen colour space or colour library, with the base colour clearly marked
  • The colour-wheel values for each colour in the harmony – Munsell notation, OKLCH, LCH, HSL, RYB or Hering’s unique hues
  • Lighter and darker tones in equal or easing steps, with values in the chosen colour model
  • Tone scale in Studio, next to Colour and Harmony: steps 50–950 with the same contrast for every hue – step 400 holds at least 3:1 and step 600 at least 4.5:1 against white, and each step shows its contrast with white and black text. Add the scale to a palette or copy it to other apps

Gradients

Create transitions between two colours in equal perceptual steps in OKLab, with lighter and darker rows.

  • Export as a CSS gradient in oklab with an sRGB fallback
  • Linear, radial or conic
  • Save whole gradients and pick up where you left off
  • Add gradients to palettes
  • Copy the gradient straight into design, layout and presentation apps – as an editable gradient, not an image
  • The tones are shown in the swatches with values in the chosen colour model and colour space
  • Drag the base tones on the lightness scale to make the whole gradient lighter or darker
  • Save, share and copy the whole set of lighter and darker tones, right from any colour in it

Getting colours

Pick colours

  • Camera with zoom, macro focus and torch – and a light panel on the Mac screen as a light source
  • Photos and images: zoom and dominant colours
  • Pick with the camera or from a photo right from the colour fields – including text and background in the contrast check
  • Screen eyedropper on Mac – also with ⌘I
  • Compensate picked colours for the light with a grey card or reference card – in photos too (beta) Read more

Apple Intelligence on device

  • From value words to a palette, grounded in a knowledge base of more than a hundred colour concepts – nature, materials, styles and values
  • Palettes are built on harmony principles with legible contrast
  • Adjust with free text – “warmer”, “more exclusive”
  • Name colours and get a critique of your palette

Everything runs on device. Nothing leaves it.

Contrast and colour vision

Text and graphics (WCAG 2.2)

  • WCAG 2.2 contrast: AA and AAA, large text and graphics
  • Contrast check by WCAG 2.2 or LRV: the figure and the strictest requirement the colour does not meet are shown at the top (for WCAG: the AA requirements), and you pick text and background right in the colour field
  • Auto-fix changes the lightness until the colour passes – also by LRV
  • Text contrast for whole palettes, right in the palette view
  • The critique shows what it is based on
  • The difference between two colours with ΔE2000, with ΔL*, ΔC* and Δh and how visible the difference is – pick the colours right in the colour fields

Surfaces and buildings (LRV)

  • Light reflectance value (LRV) and contrast between surfaces for buildings and interiors, with a choice of method: LRV difference (BS 8300), Weber (Norwegian TEK17, NS 11001) or Michelson (ISO 21542)
  • LRV and luminance contrast in the chosen light – including fluorescent and LED
  • LRV from the camera without a grey card or reference card is marked as indicative, with advice on measuring more reliably
  • Palette critique in different light: colours that change character, and colour pairs that become hard to tell apart

Colour vision

  • See the palette with protan, deutan and tritan deficiencies and achromatopsia, at any severity – and which colours become hard to tell apart
  • Colour vision deficiencies listed by how common they are, with estimated prevalence
  • Camera with a colour vision filter: see your surroundings as they may appear with each deficiency

Light and colour measurement

Simulate colours and palettes in other viewing conditions, and compensate picked colours for the light they were photographed in. A grey card or a reference card turns your phone into a simple measuring tool for colour and light.

  • See colours and whole palettes in viewing conditions and standard viewing conditions – palettes right in the palette view
  • Compensate picked colours for the light with a grey card or reference card – in photos too (beta)
  • Measure the light with the camera and save it as a viewing condition – the illuminance is estimated, or measured with a card
  • Measure colour temperature, illuminance and estimated colour rendering
  • Import the reference values for your own card

Palettes, export and sharing

Palettes and iCloud

Collect colours in palettes, save single colours and whole gradients. Name them, move colours between palettes, and critique and check contrast for whole palettes.

  • Gradients in palettes
  • Text colours in the palette if you want them: one near white and one near black, tinted with the palette’s hue. Each colour gets the one that reads best and reaches at least 4.5:1, or the one you choose
  • Turn a palette into a design system: roles for accent, neutral and status in light and dark mode, with and without increased contrast. Swap which colour has which role, and add your own roles, such as info or offers
  • “Show colour” in the menu on swatches opens the colour in Studio, and “Edit in Studio …” on the colour fields in Assess lets you adjust the colour there and bring it back
  • Gather palettes in groups that can be collapsed – drag palettes in, in the order you want
  • Print, save and copy whole palette groups – ASE files get one colour group per palette
  • Import ASE files as palettes – the colour groups in the file become a palette group
  • Reorder the colours in a palette by dragging them
  • Gather all picked colours into a new palette in one step
  • Add a colour you have just copied, right in the list
  • See whole palettes in a viewing condition, right in the palette view
  • Add the active colour right in the list
  • Print palettes on A4 – CIELab swatches with names and values
  • Undo what you do
  • Choose the order of the panels, and collapse the ones you don’t use

Everything syncs through your own private iCloud.

Export

ASE, ACO, design tokens (DTCG JSON), SVG swatches, CSS, GPL, SwiftUI, hex list and a colour list for the colour picker on the Mac.

Gradients: add gradients to palettes, and copy them as editable gradients to design, layout, office and presentation apps, or as CSS and SwiftUI.

Print: palettes as A4 PDFs with swatches in CIELab (D50), names, the values in the colour model each colour was made in, the chosen colour space or colour library and the CIELab value. Harmonies, gradients, lighter and darker tones and palette groups can be printed too.

Colours are exported in the format they were saved in – for example CMYK as CMYK. The palette’s text colours come along.

Design system: see the colours on buttons, fields, switches, alerts and tabs, with the contrast for each colour pair and the WCAG requirement it must meet. Export as colour sets for app development (light, dark and increased contrast), design tokens with aliases and one file per mode, and CSS with light-dark(). Share the design system as a link: recipients see the components in all four modes and every token in their browser, and can open it in Kolorist.

Copy to … design, layout, image, office and presentation apps, CSS or SwiftUI – in the format each app accepts. Also RGB values for CAD, BIM, video and game engines, linear values for 3D apps, and on the Mac straight into the colour picker in every app. Works for single colours and whole palettes. Choose which apps are shown, and in what order.

On Mac, drag swatches straight into other apps – and drag colours into Kolorist from the colour panel and colour wells in other apps

Colour libraries: import your own colour charts in ASE, ACO or ACB with named tones, and show the nearest tone in the chart – colours, tones and harmonies then stay on the chart’s tones.

Save as

  • Choose formats by where the files will be used – design apps, the web, app development and more – and save several at once
  • Save to a folder you choose, including cloud services – or share the files by email, messages and nearby sharing
  • For palettes and single colours

Share links

  • Share single colours, palettes, gradients and harmonies as a link
  • With Kolorist the link opens in the app, and you decide whether to save anything
  • Without the app – on PCs too – the colours are shown in the browser, with hex, OKLCH, CIELab and Munsell
  • The colours are in the link itself; nothing is stored or sent through the website
  • Tone names from colour charts you have imported are left out – only the colour values are shared
  • Open a shared example palette – and see how sharing works

Siri and Shortcuts

  • Create a palette from value words
  • Create a gradient
  • Convert a colour
  • Check contrast

iPhone, iPad and Mac

Kolorist is designed for iPhone first. On iPad and Mac you get more room – in landscape on iPad and in a wide Mac window the colour swatches sit beside the tools – and on the Mac an eyedropper that picks colours from anywhere on screen. On Mac and large iPads your palettes are at hand while you work, and you move colours where you need them with drag and drop. Colours you pick with the camera or from photos are kept – clearly marked as not saved – until you decide.
