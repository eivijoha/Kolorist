Kolorist for

colour visualisation

New in 1.4 Colours are three-dimensional. See them that way: in 3D, against the colour spaces they will be reproduced in, with the limits visible as you work – and in your physical surroundings through AR (augmented reality).

See colours in 3D

Harmonies, palettes and tone paths as points in a colour space you can rotate – in a colour sphere, in OKLab or in CIELab.

  • The colour sphere: hue around the grey axis, lightness up and saturation out towards the surface
  • OKLab, the space Kolorist computes gradients and tones in, and CIELab, the space ICC profiles use
  • The tone path with the path itself – see how the hue turns while the lightness changes
  • Live 3D view of the selected colours: next to the tools, the model follows the colours as you adjust them
  • Drag to rotate and pinch to zoom, on iPhone, iPad and Mac

Compare colour spaces

See the colour space of an ICC profile in its real shape, and lay another space over it as glass.

  • Built-in spaces – sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB, Rec. 2020, ProPhoto RGB, Generic CMYK – and your own profiles
  • Choose the rendering intent: perceptual, relative colorimetric, saturation or absolute colorimetric
  • Colours outside the space get an arrow to where they end up, for example in the print profile
  • Opened right from “Show as” in Studio

Understand gradients

Why a gradient in OKLab looks even, while an ordinary gradient often turns muddy in the middle.

  • The path through OKLab, which Kolorist uses, is a straight line
  • The path straight through sRGB, as in ordinary CSS gradients, bends and gets darker in the middle
  • The tones from both, side by side

See the limits as you work

The limit of the colour space you have chosen under “Show as”, right on the sliders and in the lightness and saturation field.

  • Stripes on the sliders where the colour falls outside – with a mark where the limit is
  • The limit drawn in the field for monochromatic harmonies and tone paths
  • A warning and the distance (ΔE00) when the colour is outside

Colour vision in the chromaticity diagram

The confusion lines show which colours can be confused with colour vision deficiencies.

  • The palette’s colours in the CIE 1931 diagram, with the line through each colour for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia
  • The pairs that become hard to tell apart, joined in red
  • Together with the simulation of how the palette looks

Read about confusion lines →

Take the model with you

Share the 3D view as a model and see the colours in your physical surroundings through AR.

  • Share as a 3D model (USDZ)
  • Place it on the table with AR on iPhone and iPad, or view the 3D model on the Mac
  • On the Mac the 3D view opens in a window of its own, next to the palette
  • Colour libraries as a point cloud – more than 2,200 filament colours – with your colour and the nearest tone
