Kolorist

Build colour palettes across colour spaces – with perceptually even gradients, contrast checking, ICC profiles and export to the tools you already use.

New in 1.3 A contrast check by WCAG, APCA or LRV, monochromatic palettes, and colours and gradients you copy straight into your design apps

Features

What’s new in version 1.3 is marked with New in 1.3

Kolorist is designed for iPhone first. On iPad and Mac you get more room – in landscape on iPad and in a wide Mac window the colour swatches sit beside the tools – and on the Mac an eyedropper that picks colours from anywhere on screen. On Mac and large iPads your palettes are at hand while you work, and you move colours where you need them with drag and drop. Colours you pick with the camera or from photos are kept – clearly marked as not saved – until you decide.

  • Every colour space

    Edit colours in OKLCH, OKLab, CIE LCH, CIELab, Munsell, HSB, HSL, RGB and CMYK – with Display P3 as the wide gamut.

    • Display P3 side by side with sRGB, Adobe RGB, Rec. 2020, ProPhoto RGB, CMYK or any installed ICC profile
    • Choose the source colour space for CMYK and RGB – Generic CMYK and sRGB by default, or any ICC profile New in 1.3
    • Save colours in their colour model or profile – exports use the same format
    • Munsell in the steps of the Munsell book – hue 2.5, value 1 and chroma 2 – with Munsell notation
    • Warnings when a colour is out of gamut
    • Optionally limit colours to a chosen profile
    • Clean CMYK values: grey components move to black (UCR/GCR), with as few inks as possible
    • Viewing light: D50 (ICC standard), D65 or one of the viewing conditions New in 1.3
    • Absolute colorimetric keeps the paper white from the CMYK profile – 0/0/0/0 shows as the paper New in 1.3
    • CMYK in percent, and RGB as 0–255 in sRGB – the same numbers as in hex – and 0–1 in other colour spaces New in 1.3

  • ICC profiles and colour libraries

    Import your own ICC profiles (.icc, .icm) and colour libraries (.ase, .aco, .acb). They live in the app’s iCloud Drive folder and follow you to your other devices.

    • Convert between profiles with a chosen rendering intent
    • Investigate colour difference with ΔE2000
    • Colour charts with named tones: work within the chart, with the tone names shown
    • Import profiles and colour charts together, and delete them again in the app

  • Gradients

    Create transitions between two colours in equal perceptual steps in OKLab, with lighter and darker rows.

    • Export as a CSS gradient in oklab with an sRGB fallback
    • Linear, radial or conic
    • Save whole gradients and pick up where you left off
    • Add gradients to palettes
    • Copy the gradient straight into design, layout and presentation apps – as an editable gradient, not an image New in 1.3
    • The tones are shown in the swatches with values in the chosen colour model and colour space
    • Drag the base tones on the lightness scale to make the whole gradient lighter or darker
    • Save, share and copy the whole set of lighter and darker tones, right from any colour in it New in 1.3

  • Tones and harmonies

    Tone scales from 50 to 950, and colour harmonies:

    • Complementary, split complementary, analogous, triad, square, double complementary and even distribution with a chosen count
    • Monochromatic: one hue in tones along a line you shape freely in lightness and saturation New in 1.3
    • Tone path: tones along a path through lightness, saturation and hue – the hue arcs between two end points New in 1.3
    • Analogous with a complementary accent – a calm group and one colour that stands out
    • Natural lightness order: yellows lighter and blues darker, as in nature – or reversed for deliberate tension
    • On OKLCH, CIE LCH, HSL or RYB colour wheels
    • The Munsell colour wheel with real Munsell colours, and Hering’s opponent-colour wheel
    • Goethe’s colour wheel from his Theory of Colours (1810): purple, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet New in 1.3
    • Harmonies ordered from calm to high-contrast: one hue, neighbours, opposite hues and evenly around the wheel New in 1.3
    • Saturation and lightness for the whole harmony, shown right in the colour wheel
    • See the whole harmony in the chosen colour space or colour library, with the base colour clearly marked
    • The colour-wheel values for each colour in the harmony – Munsell notation, OKLCH, LCH, HSL, RYB or Hering’s unique hues
    • Lighter and darker tones in equal or easing steps, with values in the chosen colour model

  • Pick colours

    • Camera with zoom, macro focus and torch – and a light panel on the Mac screen as a light source
    • Photos and images: zoom and dominant colours
    • Pick with the camera or from a photo right from the colour fields – including text and background in the contrast check New in 1.3
    • Screen eyedropper on Mac – also with ⌘I New in 1.3
    • Compensate picked colours for the light with a grey card or reference card – in photos too (beta) Read more

  • Apple Intelligence on device

    • From value words to a palette, grounded in a knowledge base of more than a hundred colour concepts – nature, materials, styles and values
    • Describe a colour – “deep ocean blue”, “dusty pink” – and see it in Studio
    • Palettes are built on harmony principles with legible contrast
    • Adjust with free text – “warmer”, “more exclusive”
    • Name colours and get a critique of your palette

    Everything runs on device. Nothing leaves it.

  • Accessibility

    • WCAG 2.2 contrast: AA and AAA, large text and graphics
    • Text on colour fields in black or white by perceived legibility (APCA), mid-tones included New in 1.3
    • Reading contrast by APCA (Lc), with what the contrast is good for: body text, headlines or graphics New in 1.3
    • Contrast check by WCAG 2.2, APCA or LRV: the figure and the strictest requirement the colour does not meet are shown at the top (for WCAG: the AA requirements), and you pick text and background right in the colour field New in 1.3
    • Auto-fix changes the lightness until the colour passes – also by APCA and LRV
    • Text contrast for whole palettes, right in the palette view
    • The critique shows what it is based on
    • Light reflectance value (LRV) and contrast between surfaces for buildings and interiors, with a choice of method: LRV difference (BS 8300), Weber (Norwegian TEK17, NS 11001) or Michelson (ISO 21542) New in 1.3
    • LRV and luminance contrast in the chosen light – including fluorescent and LED
    • LRV from the camera without a grey card or reference card is marked as indicative, with advice on measuring more reliably New in 1.3
    • Palette critique in different light: colours that change character, and colour pairs that become hard to tell apart
    • The difference between two colours with ΔE2000
    • See the palette with protan, deutan and tritan deficiencies and achromatopsia, at any severity – and which colours become hard to tell apart
    • Colour vision deficiencies listed by how common they are, with estimated prevalence
    • Camera with a colour vision filter: see your surroundings as they may appear with each deficiency

  • Palettes and iCloud

    Collect colours in palettes, save single colours and whole gradients. Name them, move colours between palettes, and critique and check contrast for whole palettes.

    • Gradients in palettes
    • Gather palettes in groups that can be collapsed – drag palettes in, in the order you want New in 1.3
    • Print, save and copy whole palette groups – ASE files get one colour group per palette New in 1.3
    • Import ASE files as palettes – the colour groups in the file become a palette group New in 1.3
    • Reorder the colours in a palette by dragging them New in 1.3
    • Gather all picked colours into a new palette in one step New in 1.3
    • Add a colour you have just copied, right in the list New in 1.3
    • See whole palettes in a viewing condition, right in the palette view
    • Add the active colour right in the list
    • Print palettes on A4 – CIELab swatches with names and values
    • Undo what you do
    • Choose the order of the panels, and collapse the ones you don’t use

    Everything syncs through your own private iCloud.

  • Export

    ASE, ACO, design tokens (DTCG JSON), SVG swatches, CSS, GPL, SwiftUI and hex list.

    Gradients: add gradients to palettes, and copy them as editable gradients to design, layout, office and presentation apps, or as CSS and SwiftUI.

    Print: palettes as A4 PDFs with swatches in CIELab (D50), names, the values in the colour model each colour was made in, the chosen colour space or colour library and the CIELab value. Harmonies, gradients, lighter and darker tones and palette groups can be printed too. New in 1.3

    Colours are exported in the format they were saved in – for example CMYK as CMYK.

    Copy to … design, layout, image, office and presentation apps, CSS or SwiftUI – in the format each app accepts. Works for single colours and whole palettes. Choose which apps are shown, and in what order. New in 1.3

    On Mac, drag swatches straight into other apps – and drag colours into Kolorist from the colour panel and colour wells in other apps

    Colour libraries: import your own colour charts in ASE, ACO or ACB with named tones, and show the nearest tone in the chart – colours, tones and harmonies then stay on the chart’s tones.

    • Create a palette from value words

    • Convert a colour
    • Check contrast
    • Describe a colour

Advanced

Light and colour measurement

Kolorist simulates how colours and whole palettes appear in other viewing conditions, and compensates picked colours for the light they were photographed in – so evening light or fluorescent tubes don’t tint the result. A grey card or a reference card turns your phone into a simple measuring tool for colour and light.

  • See colours in light

    Save viewing conditions – the living room in the evening, the office, the shop – or use standard viewing conditions for appraising prints and images (ISO 3664), workplaces and schools (EN 12464-1) and museums (CIE 157).

    • As the eye sees it: the eye adapts to the light, and in dim light colours look less vivid
    • As a photo: the full colour cast of the light
    • The colour on screen and in the light side by side, and in several viewing conditions at once
    • Whole palettes right in the palette view: the screen colour and the colour in the light in each tile, and colour pairs that become hard to tell apart
    • Bars show how well each viewing condition is known – from known spectrum to estimated
    • LRV and luminance contrast in the chosen light, for buildings and interiors
    • Fluorescent and LED lights are calculated from their spectra

  • Compensation for the light (beta)

    Compensate the colours you pick for the light they were photographed in, so you get the colour the surface has in daylight.

    • With a grey card or reference card for the right colour and lightness
    • In photos too: a card in the image is enough

  • The grey card as a yardstick

    An inexpensive grey card (18 %) or a white card gives both the colour and the strength of the light. The lightness comes out right, and you get colour temperature and illuminance (lux).

    • Custom card: use a grey or white surface with a known LRV, such as a painted wall

  • Reference cards for the highest accuracy

    With a 24-patch reference card in the image, Kolorist finds the card by itself, calibrates the camera against the reference values and shows how accurate the result is.

    • Calibrate the camera once – after that a grey card is enough, even in different light
    • Estimated colour rendering and the probable type of light
    • Accuracy given as mean and maximum ΔE00, cross-validated

  • Measure and document the light

    Colour temperature, illuminance and estimated colour rendering – useful when lighting conditions need documenting, or comparing with recommended levels, such as 500 lx at a workplace.

    • Measure the light with the camera where you are – the illuminance is estimated, or measured with a card
    • Save measured light as a viewing condition, and see other colours under it

  • Your own reference values

    Reference values for cards are not included with the app; you import the file that belongs to your card.

    • CGATS, CxF3, CSV/TSV and tables of Lab, XYZ or spectral values
    • Colour libraries (ASE, ACO, ACB)
    • With spectra, the reference values are calculated in any light

  • On Mac

    See colours and palettes in light, with the viewing conditions you measured on iPhone. With iPhone as the camera (Continuity), colours can be compensated with a grey card or reference card.

What do I need?

  • Nothing extra to see colours and palettes in light.
  • A grey or white card for compensation and light measurement.
  • A 24-patch reference card and the file with its reference values for the highest accuracy.
  • Light measurement requires iPhone or iPad.

How accurate is it?

A phone camera is not a measuring instrument. With a reference card the error is typically around 2–3 ΔE00 – the app shows the figure for each calibration. Illuminance and colour rendering are estimates, and colours without a measured spectrum get an estimated one.
